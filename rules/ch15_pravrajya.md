> **Framing note for the modern reader:** The classical "ascetic yoga" (Pravrajya) describes a chart in which four or more powerful planets gather in a single sign — a striking concentration of force that the tradition reads as a life given over to a single path. For the modern reader this is rendered as a **single-minded-vocation / focused-life tendency**: dedication to one discipline, order, or calling rather than a prediction of literal renunciation. The seven classical orders (Sakya, Ajivika, Bhikshuka, Vriddhasravaka, Chakra, Nirgrandha, Vanyasana) name the *flavour* of that path, keyed to whichever planet among the group is strongest. A planet that "suffers defeat in conjunction" (planetary war / combustion-style defeat) signals a path one may step into and then step back out of — a vocation tried rather than held for life.

## R-15.1 — Four-plus powerful planets in one sign: the focused-life yoga

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 1
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** Four or more powerful planets occupy a single rasi (sign). "Powerful" per the commentator's strength criterion; if no planet in the group is powerful, the yoga does not form. With 4+ planets counted, identify the most powerful planet of the group.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A remarkable convergence of energies marks this chart — four or more strong planets fused into one sign. The classical reading is a Pravrajya yoga: a life oriented around a single calling rather than the usual many-threaded life. This is not a doom; it is a concentration that, when honoured, becomes a gift of focus and depth. The nature of the path is set by which planet among the group is strongest (see R-4.2). If the strongest planet of the group has suffered defeat in conjunction (planetary war), the path is likely to be embraced for a season and then released — the person returns to their prior mode of life; this is a vocation tried rather than lifelong, and the constructive framing is to treat the focused period as formative rather than binding.

## R-15.3 — Defeated-in-conjunction strongest planet: vocation tried then released

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 1
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** R-4.1 has matched, AND the most powerful planet of the group has suffered defeat in conjunction (planetary war / combustion-style defeat) at birth.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** The same concentration gives a focused-life calling, but because the strongest planet has taken a defeat in conjunction, the path is embraced for a time and then set down — the person reverts to their previous condition of life. The constructive framing: this is a vocation that shapes rather than binds; the formative season on the path is real and is kept, even though the outer form is eventually released. Pair with R-4.1's gift of focus as the offsetting strength.

## R-15.4 — Strongest planet astangata: drawn to the order without formal entry

- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** R-4.1 has matched, AND the most powerful yoga planet is an Astangata planet (defeated / set / combust — see translator's note pointing to Ch 4, v 2 notes).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** When the strongest planet of the yoga is astangata, the person does not formally become a Deekshita (one of the holy order), yet is drawn to and attached to those who are. ## R-15.5 — Defeated-in-conjunction strongest planet aspected by others: desire without entry

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 2
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** R-4.1 has matched, AND the most powerful planet of the group has suffered defeat in conjunction, AND that planet is aspected by other planets.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** In this configuration the person does not formally enter the order, yet carries a strong desire to do so — a calling felt keenly but not enacted in outer form. The offsetting strength is the sincerity of the pull itself, which can be honoured by supporting the path from one's station in life. ## R-15.6 — Moon's sign-lord and Saturn mutual aspect: the focused-life path

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 3
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** Either (A) the lord of the rasi occupied by the Moon aspects Saturn AND that lord is not aspected by any other planet; OR (B) Saturn aspects the lord of the sign occupied by the Moon when that lord is not powerful.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A second route to the focused-life yoga: when the Moon's sign-lord and Saturn stand in mutual aspect under the conditions above, the chart gives a Pravrajya tendency. The flavour follows whichever of the two — the Moon's sign-lord or Saturn — is powerful (case A), or Saturn (case B). This is rendered as a serious inclination toward a single-minded path; the offsetting strength is Saturn's capacity for sustained discipline and the Moon's sign-lord's grounding in one's emotional nature.

## R-15.7 — Moon in Saturn's Drekkana and Saturn/Mars Navamsa aspected by Saturn: the focused-life path

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 3
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** The Moon occupies the Drekkana of Saturn, AND the Moon occupies the Navamsa of Saturn or Mars, AND the Moon is aspected by Saturn, AND the Moon is not aspected by any other planet (per translator's footnote (d)).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A third route to the focused-life yoga: the Moon seated in Saturn's Drekkana and in a Saturn-or-Mars Navamsa, aspected by Saturn alone, gives a Pravrajya tendency of the Saturn flavour (Nirgrandha — the naked-renunciate / austere order; see R-4.2). For the modern reader this is a serious inclination toward austerely disciplined solitude and contemplation; the offsetting strength is Saturn's gift of endurance and depth, and the dignified close is that such a temperament, given a worthy object, sustains what others cannot.

## R-15.8 — Raja yoga + ascetic yoga: scientific author

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 4
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** Jupiter, the Moon, and the rising sign (Ascendant) are all aspected by Saturn; AND Jupiter occupies the 9th house from the Ascendant; AND the chart contains a Raja yoga; AND (per the verse's pairing) an ascetic yoga (R-4.1 or R-4.6/R-4.7) is also present.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** Under a Raja yoga combined with an ascetic yoga, when Saturn aspects Jupiter, the Moon and the lagna, and Jupiter sits in the ninth house, the chart gives a scientific author — a person of learning who writes authoritatively. Per the translator's note, when both a Raja yoga and an ascetic yoga are present, the ascetic yoga takes effect and the Raja yoga does not, as in the lives of Varahamihira, Kanada, Buddha, Panchasikha, Brahmagupta and others; where two Raja yogas and an ascetic yoga are present, the person becomes both a king and a scientific author, as Janaka, Kasiraja, Suchidhwaja and others.

## R-15.9 — Saturn in 9th unaspected under Raja yoga: emperor and ascetic

- **Cites:** Ch 15, v 4
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** Saturn occupies the 9th house from the Ascendant; AND Saturn is not aspected by any other planet; AND the chart contains a Raja yoga.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** When Saturn sits in the ninth house untouched by any other aspect, under a Raja yoga, the chart gives one who is both an emperor and an ascetic — a sovereign of outward dominion who inwardly walks a single-minded path. For the modern reader this is the rare combination of worldly authority and focused-life vocation: leadership held in service of a calling rather than for its own sake. The dignified close is that power, so ordered, becomes an instrument of the path.
