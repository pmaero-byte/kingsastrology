## R-12.1 — Nabhasa group counts

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 1
- **Classification:** table
- **Condition:** Static lookup. The Nabhasa yogas comprise four groups: Akriti (20 yogas), Sankhya (7), Asraya (3), Dala (2) — 32 in all. Used to validate that a matched yoga belongs to one of these four classes.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** The Nabhasa yogas fall into four families — twenty shape (Akriti) yogas, seven count (Sankhya) yogas, three foundation (Asraya) yogas, and two fold (Dala) yogas. The Yavanas treat extensively of one thousand and eight hundred such yogas; this work summarises them under these thirty-two.
- **Verse (source):** "Nine, ten and eight, multiplied respectively by three, three and four give the sums of the numbers of the first two, the first three, and all the four groups of Nabhasa yogas (a). The Yavanas treat extensively of one thousand and eight hundred of such yogas (b). We shall here summarise them (c)."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator's footnote (a) unpacks the algebraic statement: Akriti = 20, Sankhya = 7, Asraya = 3, Dala = 2. Footnote (b): per Suchidhwaja the number is unlimited though Yavanas speak of 1,800. Footnote (c): the 32 cover the effects of the 1,800.

## R-12.2 — Rajju yoga (Asraya)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 2 (definition); Ch 12, v 11 (effect)
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy movable signs (Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn) — and the arrangement does not simultaneously satisfy a higher-precedence Akriti/Dala yoga (see R-12.34).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Rajju pattern tends toward restlessness — jealous of others' wealth, drawn to foreign lands, fond of travelling. The offsetting strength is a wide horizon: movement and travel become the field through which one's life-pattern unfolds rather than a confinement.
- **Verse (source):** "A person born under a Rajju yoga will be jealous of the wealth of others, will proceed to foreign lands and will be fond of travelling"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Per Satyachariyar (footnote a to v 11). Some hold that planets must occupy all four movable signs; Garga disagrees (footnote (o) to v 2).

## R-12.3 — Musala yoga (Asraya)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 2 (definition); Ch 12, v 11 (effect)
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy fixed signs (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius) — and the arrangement does not simultaneously satisfy a higher-precedence Akriti/Dala yoga.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Musala pattern gives steadiness — respectable, rich, and engaged in various undertakings. The gift is rootedness and capacity to sustain many enterprises at once.
- **Verse (source):** "a person born under a Musala yoga will be respectable, rich, and will engage in various undertakings"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Per Satyachariyar.

## R-12.4 — Nala yoga (Asraya)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 2 (definition); Ch 12, v 11 (effect)
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy common (dual/Ubhaya) signs (Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces) — and the arrangement does not simultaneously satisfy a higher-precedence Akriti/Dala yoga.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Nala pattern tends toward a mixed frame — defective organs, settled views, rich, skilled in work. The offsetting strength is firm conviction and craftsmanship: settled views and skilled hands become the resources through which the body's limitations are transcended.
- **Verse (source):** "a person born under a Nala yoga will be of defective organs, settled views, rich and skilled in work"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Per Satyachariyar. "Defective organs" read as a tendency toward bodily sensitivity, not a sentence.

## R-12.5 — Srik / Mala yoga (Dala)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 2 (definition); Ch 12, v 11 (effect)
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** Benefic planets (Mercury, Jupiter, Venus per Garga) occupy the Kendras (houses 1, 4, 7, 10) — and no malefic occupies a Kendra (per Badarayana's reading); or per Garga, three of the Kendras hold benefics.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Srik (Mala) pattern is a gift of ease — living in comfort and luxury. The strength is a life held in generous surroundings.
- **Verse (source):** "a person born under a Srik yoga will live in comfort and luxury"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Per Garga (footnote b to v 11). Benefics = Mercury, Jupiter, Venus (footnote b to v 2). Footnote (c): Garga requires three Kendras; Badarayana requires no malefic in any Kendra.

## R-12.6 — Sarpa yoga (Dala)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 2 (definition); Ch 12, v 11 (effect)
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** Malefic planets (Sun, Mars, Saturn per Garga) occupy the Kendras (houses 1, 4, 7, 10) — and no benefic occupies a Kendra (per Badarayana's reading); or per Garga, three of the Kendras hold malefics.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Sarpa pattern tends toward misery in many ways. The offsetting strength is the forge: hardship faced consciously becomes the discipline that reshapes circumstance, and the Dala yoga's lifelong presence gives steady ground on which to build resilience.
- **Verse (source):** "one born under a Sarpa yoga will be miserable in many ways"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Per Garga. Malefics = Sun, Mars, Saturn (footnote d to v 2). Same Kendra-count variants as R-12.5.

## R-12.7 — Gada yoga (Akriti)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 4 (definition); Ch 12, v 13 (effect)
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy any two adjacent Kendras (1st & 4th; 4th & 7th; 7th & 10th; or 10th & 1st).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Gada pattern gives ritual competence and wealth — one performs sacrificial rites, is rich, and is ever engaged in the acquisition of wealth. The gift is sustained prosperity joined to religious duty.
- **Verse (source):** "A person born under a Gada yoga will perform sacrificial rites, will be rich, and will ever be engaged in the acquisition of wealth"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Footnote (a) to v 4: the Yavanas split this into four distinct yogas — Gada (1st & 4th), Sankha (4th & 7th), Vibhuka (7th & 10th), Dwaja (10th & 1st).

## R-12.8 — Sakata yoga (Akriti)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 4 (definition); Ch 12, v 13 (effect)
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy the 1st and 7th houses.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Sakata pattern tends toward a hard road — living by means of carts, afflicted with diseases, and having a mean wife. The offsetting strength is the cart itself: a livelihood of transport and movement that, handled with care for health and companionship, becomes a steady if modest trade.
- **Verse (source):** "a person born under a Sakata yoga will live by means of carts, will be afflicted with diseases, and will have a mean wife"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** "Mean wife" read as friction in partnership, not a verdict on the partner.

The offsetting strength is mobility and connectedness: the messenger who learns discretion turns travel and speech into bridges rather than friction.

## R-12.10 — Sringataka yoga (Akriti)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 4 (definition); Ch 12, v 13 (effect)
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy the 1st, 5th, and 9th houses (the trines from the ascendant).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Sringataka pattern gives late-life flourishing — happy in the latter end of life. The gift is a life that ripens.
- **Verse (source):** "a person born under a Sringataka yoga will be happy in the latter end of life"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Footnote (a) to v 13: the text word is "Chira Sukhi"; some read "happy for a long time"; Garga adds harsh speech. Flag for scholar review on which reading to freeze.

## R-12.11 — Hala yoga (Akriti)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 4 (definition); Ch 12, v 13 (effect)
- **Classification:** neutral
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy the "other triangular houses" — i.e., trines not anchored at the ascendant: per footnote (b) to v 4, the 2nd-6th-10th, or 3rd-7th-11th, or 4th-8th-12th.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Hala pattern marks a life of cultivation — one tills lands. The pattern is descriptive of an agrarian, productive vocation.
- **Verse (source):** "a person born under a Hala yoga will till lands"
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Footnote (b) to v 4 lists the three trinal sets. Condition testable as: planets occupy one of those three trinal triples.

## R-12.12 — Vajra yoga (Akriti)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 5 (definition); Ch 12, v 14 (effect)
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** Benefic planets occupy the 1st and 7th houses while malefic planets occupy the 4th and 10th houses.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Vajra pattern gives a life happy at both ends — a general favourite, bold in fight. The gift is courage paired with social grace.
- **Verse (source):** "A person born under a Vajra yoga will be happy both at the beginning and end of life, will be a general favourite and will be bold in fight."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** v 6 raises the objection that Mercury and Venus cannot occupy the 4th sign from the Sun; the translator's long note argues the yoga refers to the Bhava Chakra (houses), not the Rasi Chakra. Condition is evaluated on the Bhava Chakra. Flag for scholar review.

## R-12.15 — Vapi yoga (Akriti)

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 5 (definition); Ch 12, v 14 (effect)
- **Classification:** tend
- **Condition:** All seven planets occupy the four Panaphara houses (2nd, 5th, 8th, 11th) or the four Apoklima houses (3rd, 6th, 9th, 12th).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** A Vapi pattern tends toward meagre comfort stretched long — living in poor comfort for a long time, burying wealth underground, a miser. The offsetting strength is thrift turned to stewardship: the same conservation instinct, consciously loosened, becomes steady provision across a long life.
- **Verse (source):** "A person born under a Vapi yoga will live in poor comfort for a long time, will bury his wealth under ground and will be a miser."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Panaphara = 2, 5, 8, 11; Apoklima = 3, 6, 9, 12 (footnotes b, c to v 5).

## R-12.34 — Nabhasa precedence rule

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 3; Ch 12, v 10; Ch 12, v 12
- **Classification:** table
- **Condition:** Precedence when a chart satisfies more than one Nabhasa yoga. Order: (1) Dala yogas (Srik, Sarpa) override Sankhya coincidences (Vallaki/Damini/Pasa/Kedara) — treat as Dala. (2) Asraya yogas that partake of an Akriti yoga are treated as the Akriti (v 12). (3) Sankhya yogas that coincide with an Akriti are treated as the Akriti (v 10). (4) When Asraya and Sankhya coincide: Kedara/Sula/Yuga → treat as Asraya; Gola → treat as Gola (exception, so Gola can ever arise). Otherwise each yoga produces its own effect.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** Where one arrangement satisfies two Nabhasa patterns at once, the more specific shape (Akriti or Dala) is read; the count (Sankhya) and foundation (Asraya) yield to it. The single exception is Gola, which must be read as Gola whenever it applies, so that the one-sign pattern is never lost.
- **Verse (source):** "If the Asraya yogas partake at the same time of the character of other yogas, they should be treated as the latter; otherwise, they produce the effects described for them."

## R-12.35 — Nabhasa effects are lifelong

- **Cites:** Ch 12, v 19
