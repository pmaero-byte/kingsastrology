## R-4.1 — Three or more exalted planets (Yavana / Jeevasarma)

- **Cites:** Ch 4, v 1
- **Classification:** strength (with a tend sub-clause)
- **Condition:** Count of planets occupying their exaltation signs ≥ 3. Sub-classify the set: (a) all benefic among them; (b) all malefic among them; (c) mixed benefic + malefic. Also evaluate the special case of count ≥ 5 exalted planets, and the case of planets in Trikona or Moola Trikona signs (per translator note).
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** Three or more planets risen to their exaltation seat gift a strong capacity for leadership. Where the exalted planets are benefic, the leadership tends toward just and beneficent rule; where they are malefic, the leadership carries a harder, more forceful edge — a tendency to dominate that, untended, can slide into tyranny, and which asks to be tempered by ethics and restraint. Where the set is mixed, both capacities are present and the native is asked to choose the benevolent current consciously. With five or more exalted planets, the leadership gift is strong enough to arise from any station of birth, not only a ruling lineage. The same fruit holds for planets occupying Trikona or Moola Trikona signs. (Jeevasarma's contrary reading — that exalted malefics make one rich, angry, and quarrelsome but not a sovereign — is preserved in Notes as a tend offset; Varahamihira follows the Yavanas.)
- **Verse (source):** "According to the Yavanas, a person born when three or more malefic planets are in their exaltation signs becomes a tyrannical king. According to Jeevasarma, if such planets be malefic, a person will never become a king."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator note (a): if the 3+ exalted planets are benefic, the person becomes a good king; if both benefic and malefic, he becomes both a good and a bad king — Manitha concurs. Note (b): Jeevasarma holds such a person becomes rich, angry, and fond of quarrels but not a king; Varahamihira follows the Yavana view and adds (per his Swalpa Jataka) that 3+ exalted planets make a king if born in a king's family, and 5+ exalted planets make a king in whatever family — the same applying to Trikona or Moola Trikona signs. The "tyrannical" framing is rendered above as a tend sub-clause (hard-edged leadership to be tempered) per the strength-before-doom guardrail. Testability: exaltation-sign lookup and benefic/malefic classification are deterministic; "Trikona / Moola Trikona" sign-sets must be supplied by the dignity table (R-* table rules).

## R-4.2 — Thirty-two Raja-yogas from Mars, Saturn, Sun, Jupiter exalted

- **Cites:** Ch 4, v 2
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** Among the four planets {Mars, Saturn, Sun, Jupiter}, count how many occupy their exaltation signs, and require that the Lagna (rising sign) be one of the exaltation signs of those exalted planets. Two families: (A) all four exalted, or any three of the four exalted, with the Lagna being one of those exalted signs → 16 yogas; (B) any two exalted, or any one exalted, with the Lagna being one of those exalted signs AND the Moon occupying sign Cancer → 16 yogas. Match if any one of these 32 configurations holds.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** When Mars, Saturn, the Sun, and Jupiter rise into their exaltation seats and the ascendant is one of those exalted signs, a strong sovereignty-capacity is conferred — and when the Moon rests in Cancer alongside two or even one of these exalted planets with such an ascendant, the same gift is conferred. These are the thirty-two classical Raja-yogas of this verse; any one of them marks a capacity for rulership, ethical strength, and enduring influence to be developed and used for the welfare of others.
- **Verse (source):** "Of the four planets Mars, Saturn, the Sun and Jupiter, for all the four or any three of them occupying exaltation signs, while each of the planets occupies the Lagna, the number of Raja-yogas is 16. Again, of the same four planets, for any two or for any one occupying exaltation signs and in each case for each of the planets occupying the Lagna while the Moon occupies sign Cancer, the number of Raja-yogas is also 16."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator note details the 32-yoga count: (A) 4 (all four exalted, each exalted sign rising) + 12 (4 groups of three, 3 rising signs each) = 16; (B) 12 (6 groups of two, 2 rising signs each, Moon in Cancer) + 4 (each single planet exalted and rising, Moon in Cancer) = 16. Exaltation signs: Sun=Aries, Jupiter=Cancer, Saturn=Libra, Mars=Capricorn. The Lagna-in-exaltation-sign constraint makes each configuration astronomically testable.

## R-4.3 — Forty-four Vargottama Raja-yogas (planets aspecting Lagna or Moon)

- **Cites:** Ch 4, v 3
- **Classification:** strength
- **Condition:** Lagna in a Vargottama position (same sign in Rasi and Navamsa) OR the Moon in a Vargottama position. Then, of the six planets {Sun, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn} (Moon excluded), require that all members of at least one of the following groups aspect the Lagna (if Lagna is Vargottama) or the Moon (if Moon is Vargottama): any group of 4 of the six (15 groups), any group of 5 (6 groups), or all 6 (1 group) → 22 groups; each evaluated for Lagna-aspect and for Moon-aspect → 44 yogas. Match if any one holds. (Per Mandavya, multiply by 12 signs — see Notes.)
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** When the ascendant or the Moon stands firm in a Vargottama station, and a full set of four, five, or six planets (besides the Moon) casts its aspect upon that stable point, a sovereignty-capacity is conferred. The gift is one of integrated, many-sided leadership — the more planets that aspect, the broader the support — to be wielded with the steadiness that Vargottama placement grants.
- **Verse (source):** "The Lagna or the Moon being in Vargottama positions, of all the planets excepting the Moon, for the several groups of four, five and six planets aspecting the Lagna or the Moon, the number of yogas obtained in each case is 22."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator note: 44 Raja-yogas are described — 22 groups (15 of four + 6 of five + 1 of six) aspecting the Vargottama Lagna give 22 yogas, and the same 22 groups aspecting the Vargottama Moon give another 22. Mandavya multiplies by 12 (each of the 12 signs as Lagna or Moon-sign) for 44 × 12. Aspect rules (planetary drishti) and Vargottama computation (Rasi = Navamsa sign) must be supplied by the chart engine. "Excepting the Moon" is explicit in the verse.

- **Verse (source):** "When the Moon occupies the 10th house, Saturn the 11th house, Jupiter the rising sign, Mercury and Mars the 2nd house, and the Sun and Venus occupy the 4th house, a person born becomes a king if he belongs to a king's family. Again, when Mars and Saturn occupy the rising sign, when the Moon occupies the 4th house, Jupiter the 7th house, Venus the 9th house, the Sun the 10th house, and Mercury the 11th house, a person born becomes a king if he belongs to a king's family."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator notes: (a) and (b) — and only rich, if otherwise. The Lagna sign is not fixed in either sub-case; houses are counted from whatever the Lagna is. Fully testable given a computed Lagna and house assignment.

## R-4.19 — Dasa periods: gaining and losing kingdom

- **Cites:** Ch 4, v 19
- **Classification:** tend (paired with strength)
- **Condition:** Evaluate the planet(s) whose Dasa (or Antardasa) is running. (A) If the Dasa lord occupies the 10th house or the Lagna → Raja-yoga is produced in that period → kingdom/leadership obtained. If there are planets in both Lagna and 10th, or several in either, the Antardasa of the most powerful among them produces the yoga. If there are no planets in Lagna or 10th, the Dasa of the most powerful planet produces the yoga. (B) If the Dasa (or Antardasa) lord occupies an inimical sign or a depression (debilitation) sign → the kingdom/leadership is lost and the native courts the friendship of another king. Sub-classify (B): if such a Dasa lord is powerful → permanent loss; if not powerful → recovery with the help of allies.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** Leadership-capacity fruited through planetary periods (Dasas): in the Dasa or Antardasa of a planet placed in the tenth house or the ascendant — or, failing those, of the most powerful planet — sovereignty and authority come into one's hands. In the Dasa or Antardasa of a planet fallen into an inimical or debilitated sign, that authority is lost and one is led to seek the alliance of another ruler. Where the causing planet is weak, the loss is temporary and recoverable through allies; where it is powerful in a wrong placement, the loss runs deeper. This is a rhythm of rising and receding — not a verdict — and the native is invited to align preparation with the strong periods and to lean on alliances in the receding ones.
- **Verse (source):** "Of the planets producing a Rajayoga either in the Dasa periods of those which occupy the 10th house or the Lagna or in that of the most powerful planet, a person obtains kingdom and he loses it and courts the friendship of another king, in the Dasa periods of the planets occupying inimical or depression signs."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator notes: (a) Dasa or Antardasa periods per the Commentator; (b) if planets occupy both Lagna and 10th, or several in either, then in the Antardasa of the most powerful of them; (c) if there are no planets in Lagna or 10th, then the Dasa of the most powerful planet; (d) Dasa or Antardasa per the Commentator; (e) if such planets be powerful, the king loses his kingdom permanently, and if not powerful, he recovers it with the help of allies. Requires Dasa/Antardasa computation, inimical-sign and debilitation-sign tables, and a strength metric (flag for scholar review).

## R-4.20 — Comfort-luxury yoga and hunter/robber-chief yoga

- **Cites:** Ch 4, v 20
- **Classification:** strength (with a neutral-to-tend sub-clause)
- **Condition:** (A) Jupiter in Lagna AND Venus in Lagna AND Mercury in Lagna (commentator: Lagna = Aries) AND Saturn in 7th house AND Sun in 10th house → lives in great comfort and luxury (regardless of poverty or other yogas). (B) Powerful benefic signs form the Kendras (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th houses occupied by / as benefic signs) AND malefic planets occupy malefic signs → becomes rich and chief of hunters or robbers. Match if (A) or (B) holds.
- **Effect (one-way, dignity-closing):** The first arrangement gifts a life of great comfort and luxury — and notably this gift holds even if the native is otherwise poor or under a different yoga, so the reader need not fear that other placements cancel it. The second arrangement gifts a richness together with a leadership among a fierce or marginal company — a capacity to head a band of hunters or robbers; this is rendered not as doom but as a strong, wild, out-of-mainstream leadership current that asks to be redirected toward protective, lawful, skillful ends (e.g. stewardship of wilds, security, frontier defense).
- **Verse (source):** "When Jupiter, Venus and Mercury occupy the rising sign, when Saturn occupies the 7th house and the Sun the 10th house, a person born will live in great comfort and luxury. Again, when powerful benefic signs form the Kendras and malefic planets occupy malefic signs, a person born becomes rich and the chief of hunters or robbers."
- **Status:** draft
- **Notes:** Translator notes are extensive. (a) The commentator takes the rising sign as Aries; however, some object that when Venus and Mercury (inferior planets) occupy the rising sign, the Sun cannot occupy the 10th house (more than 60° from them, exceeding their greatest elongations of 41° and 29°). An alternate interpretation is proposed: the rising sign is a house of Jupiter, Venus, or Mercury. Bhatta Utpala grants the objection but says the literal reading has the support of Garga, whom he quotes. Varahamihira himself admits the impossibility but states his task is only to report the ancient writers (see also notes to Stanza 6, Chapter XIII — here Chapter 4). (b) Comfort-luxury holds though the native may be poor and under whatever yoga. (c) "benefic signs" = signs of benefic planets. (d) Some interpret "powerful benefic signs form the Kendras" as "powerful benefic planets occupy the Kendras," but this is opposed to Garga per the commentator. The astronomical-impossibility flag on sub-case (A) is a scholar-review item; the rule is left draft.
